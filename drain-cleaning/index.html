Drain Cleaning in Leander, TX

We clear slow, backed-up drains across Leander and nearby communities.

Call (512) 563-3818 Ask a question

A kitchen sink that gurgles after the tap stops, a shower that holds water at your feet, or a sour odor from a floor drain usually starts the call. We trace those signs through the trap, branch pipe, and accessible cleanout instead of treating the smell alone. Food residue, soap film, hair, and paper can narrow different sections of the line and change how water leaves the fixture.

Water may drain slowly in one bathroom while a nearby tub bubbles because both fixtures meet at the same branch. We work on drain blockages in Leander, Cedar Park, Jonestown, and Liberty Hill from our S West Dr location, checking the fixture that shows the symptom and the route beyond it. A wet ring under a sink or water rising in a shower while another fixture empties gives us a useful starting point.

A single slow sink can sometimes wait while you limit use and watch its water level. A drain that sends wastewater into a tub, shower, or floor drain should not wait, because the blockage can force water out of the branch pipe and onto flooring, cabinets, or drywall.

Gurgling sinks and sewer smells

Customers often say, "my sink gurgles," "the shower will not drain," or "a sewer smell comes up after we run water." We also hear that the kitchen sink backs up into the other bowl, the toilet flush makes bubbles in the tub, or the laundry standpipe spills onto the floor. A clog near a fixture, a blocked branch, and a restriction farther along the line can produce similar sounds and water levels. We inspect before choosing a tool because those faults call for different access points and different amounts of work, even though they can sound identical over the phone.

Drain line inspection

During a drain line inspection, we begin at the affected sink, tub, shower, or floor drain and run water to watch the flow and listen for air movement. We check the trap, visible pipe joints, and available cleanout, then note the pipe diameter, the water level, and how far a cable can travel before meeting resistance. An accessible fixture check and cleanout test often take 20 to 40 minutes, while a cable that must pass farther through a branch line takes longer to clear and test.

We can often remove hair, soap buildup, food residue, or paper from an accessible drain on the same visit. If a trap washer leaks after removal, a cleanout cap has seized, or camera footage points to a damaged section of pipe, we explain the next repair before booking further work. Opening a wall, replacing a buried pipe section, or arranging access to a shared line may need a separate visit.

Cleanout access and pipe route

An exposed cleanout beside the house, in a garage, or beneath a sink usually gives us a direct route to the blockage. A drain line hidden behind a cabinet, below a slab, or beyond a fixture trap can require more setup and a different cable path. Older cast-iron pipe, root intrusion, heavy grease buildup, and repeated backups can also change the work needed to restore flow.

A removable trap lets us inspect the first section of pipe without disturbing a countertop or cabinet base. If the blockage sits farther down the route, we may need to use another cleanout or inspect the line with a camera before deciding whether cleaning alone will hold. Work that moves beyond the drain line and into walls, flooring, or a shared building connection can also require owner approval or sign-off.

Fixture and cleanout location

Before calling, note which room has the slow drain and which fixture causes another fixture to gurgle or back up. Tell us whether water is standing in the sink, tub, shower, or floor drain right now, and whether the blockage affects one fixture or several. If you can safely see a cleanout cap, let us know whether it sits outside, in a garage, under a sink, or behind an access panel.

Visible pipe material, such as PVC, ABS, or cast iron, can help us plan the right cable head and fittings. Let us know if a cabinet, parked vehicle, locked gate, or crawlspace blocks access to the cleanout.
