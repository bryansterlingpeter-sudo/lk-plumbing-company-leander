Garbage Disposal Repair in Leander, TX

We repair humming, leaking, jammed, and non-working garbage disposals.

Call (512) 563-3818 Ask a question

Older single-family kitchens in Leander, Cedar Park, Jonestown, and Liberty Hill often have a disposal that has worked through years of food scraps, detergent, and hard-water buildup beneath the sink. The motor, mounting ring, splash guard, and dishwasher drain connection all sit inside a tight sink cabinet, where a small leak or loose fitting can go unnoticed until water reaches the cabinet floor. We check the disposal and its connections before treating a humming unit as a motor failure.

A disposal can stop after a jam, a tripped reset button, a failed wall switch, or a worn motor. Water under the sink may come from the disposal housing, the mounting assembly, the dishwasher hose, or the trap beside it. A disposal that hums, leaks, smells hot, or trips a breaker should not keep running or receive repeated reset attempts, because a seized motor, damaged wiring, or loose mounting ring can worsen while the unit stays in use.

Humming sink and food odor

Customers often call because the disposal hums but the grinding plate will not turn, or because the switch does nothing and the sink fills with food scraps. A rotten food odor can come up through the splash guard even after the basin has been cleaned, especially when debris remains around the grinding chamber. Water may drip from the bottom of the disposal, run down the side of the housing, or collect beneath the mounting ring where the unit meets the sink. These symptoms can look similar from the kitchen counter, but a jammed impeller, failed motor, worn seal, blocked outlet, and loose mount each call for different parts and different work.

Under-sink disposal inspection

We start by shutting off power at the breaker, then inspect the cord or hardwired connection, wall switch, reset button, splash guard, mounting ring, and drain outlet. A meter check can confirm whether the disposal receives the expected 120-volt power supply, while a manual inspection helps identify a jammed grinding plate or foreign object in the chamber. We also check the 1 1/2-inch drain connection, trap, and dishwasher hose for water escaping into the cabinet.

A basic inspection and functional test commonly takes about 20 to 40 minutes, depending on cabinet access and whether water has reached the electrical connection. We can often clear a jam, reset the unit, tighten a mounting assembly, or replace a failed switch component during the same visit when the needed part matches the disposal. A replacement disposal, uncommon mounting part, or damaged electrical connection may need a return visit after the correct model or component has been obtained.

Motor, mounting ring, cabinet access

The disposal motor and the condition of the mounting ring move the scope of repair more than a visible drip alone suggests. An older unit with a leaking lower seal may need replacement rather than a small adjustment, while a loose mount or worn splash guard may involve less disassembly. Tight cabinet space, a built-in water filter, stored cleaning supplies, or a dishwasher hose routed across the disposal can add time before we can reach the housing and wiring. If the exact motor, sink flange, or mounting assembly must be ordered, or a rental property needs owner approval before replacement, the work may require a separate return visit.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, check the disposal model number on the label attached to the side or bottom of the housing. Note whether the unit has stopped completely, hums at the switch, leaks from the bottom, or backs water into the sink. Let us know which kitchen sink has the disposal and whether a dishwasher drain hose connects to it. If possible, clear bottles and supplies from the sink cabinet so we can reach the outlet, reset button, and mounting ring.
