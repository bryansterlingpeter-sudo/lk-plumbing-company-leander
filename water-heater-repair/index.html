Water Heater Repair in Leander, TX

We repair leaking, cold, noisy, and unreliable water heaters in Leander.

Call (512) 563-3818 Ask a question

Cold water at every hot tap and water spreading from the base of a heater often get called the same problem, but they point to different repairs. A hallway may reveal a puddle near the utility closet, while a shower may run cold with no water visible at the tank. We handle both a heater that has stopped heating and a heater that is releasing water from a valve, fitting, or tank.

We work from 1000 S West Dr and cover Leander, Cedar Park, Jonestown, and Liberty Hill. A heater producing only cold water can sometimes wait briefly while the source is checked, but water on the floor should not wait because a leaking tank or relief valve can damage flooring, framing, and nearby electrical connections.

No hot water and puddles

Customers often report “no hot water,” “hot water runs out fast,” “water around the heater,” or “the pilot will not stay lit.” An electric heater may lose heat through an element, thermostat, high-limit switch, or wiring connection. A gas heater with a pilot that drops out can involve the thermocouple, igniter, burner assembly, or gas control valve.

Popping or rumbling from the tank can come from sediment under the lower element or burner area, while rusty water may point toward corrosion inside the tank or a worn anode rod. Water at the heater may come from the temperature-pressure relief valve, a supply fitting, the drain valve, or the tank shell itself. These symptoms can look alike from the kitchen or hallway, but the failed part and the labor needed to reach it can lead to very different repair work.

Heater inspection and test

We begin the inspection at the heater, checking the model plate, fuel type, visible piping, shutoff valve, drain valve, vent connection, and floor around the base. We check for moisture at fittings before testing heat, then measure incoming water pressure; most residential plumbing runs within a range of about 40 to 80 psi. For electric units, we isolate power before testing elements, thermostats, and wiring, while gas units require checks of the pilot, burner flame, ignition sequence, and venting. A straightforward diagnostic inspection commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on access and the fault.

We can often replace a thermostat, heating element, thermocouple, relief valve, or accessible supply fitting during the same visit when the correct part is available. We test the heater after the repair and compare the outlet temperature with the control setting; 120 degrees Fahrenheit is a common household setting. A tank with a failed shell, inaccessible vent work, or a part that must be ordered may require a return visit or separate replacement work.

Anode rods and shutoffs

Tank age and the condition of the anode rod can change the scope of a repair. A heater with an accessible element panel or relief valve usually takes less strip-back than one set in a tight closet behind shelving, ductwork, or a finished wall. Corrosion around the tank seam can also shift the work away from a replaceable fitting and toward tank replacement.

A seized cold-water shutoff adds labor because we may need to address the valve before isolating the heater safely. A manufacturer-specific gas control, electronic board, or tankless component may need to be ordered rather than fitted from common stock. Permit or inspection sign-off, where required for replacement or vent changes, also affects the work planned for the heater.

Model plate and closet access

Before calling, check the manufacturer data plate on the heater and have the model and serial number available. The plate may sit on the side of the tank, behind a removable panel, or near the burner compartment. If you know the approximate installation age, tell us that along with whether the unit uses gas or electricity.

Let us know which room holds the heater, whether water is running from the base or a valve, and whether the closet, attic, or garage access has a clear path. A photo of the pipes above the heater and the floor below it can help us identify the likely fitting and bring suitable parts.
