Pipe Leak Repair in Leander, TX

We trace and repair pipe leaks before water reaches walls, cabinets, and ceilings.

Call (512) 563-3818 Ask a question

Most people start by closing the angle stop beneath a sink, turning off a fixture valve, or setting a bucket under the drip. That is a sensible first move because it can keep water off cabinet flooring while we trace the source. The approach stops helping when water comes from a wall, ceiling, slab edge, or a pipe joint that remains pressurized after the fixture valve closes. A towel catches visible water, but it cannot dry the cavity behind drywall.

We treat an active pipe leak as work that should not wait, especially when water reaches a ceiling, wall plate, cabinet base, or electrical outlet. Even a slow leak can soak insulation and drywall long after the floor looks dry. A loose connection under an open sink may allow time for a planned repair, but water inside a wall or ceiling needs attention before the wet area spreads.

Water stain and hissing

Customers often call about a brown ceiling spot, a hissing sound behind a wall, water pooling beneath a cabinet, or a water meter that keeps moving after faucets and appliances are off. Some find paint bubbling near a baseboard, while others notice a warm section of floor or a musty smell in a closet. A dripping copper joint, a split PEX line, a failed compression fitting, and a leak under a slab can look alike from the room side. We inspect the pipe route because those faults need different repairs and involve different materials, access, and labor.

Leak inspection

We begin by checking the visible pipe, fixture shutoff, meter movement, and moisture around the affected wall or cabinet. We may isolate a branch line, read water pressure, and listen at the pipe route before opening drywall. A visible supply connection can often be inspected within twenty to forty minutes, while a hidden leak takes longer to narrow down without removing unnecessary material.

On the same visit, we can often replace an exposed PEX fitting, copper coupling, valve connection, or short damaged section of pipe. We shut off the water, remove the failed fitting, install compatible material, and test the repaired line under pressure before restoring service. A leak beneath tile, inside a structural wall, or along a slab route may require a separate visit for access work, pipe rerouting, or an inspection sign-off.

Shutoffs, walls, and pipe material

Pipe material changes the repair work because copper, PEX, CPVC, galvanized steel, and PVC use different fittings, cutting tools, and connection methods. An exposed PEX coupling inside a sink cabinet usually gives us direct access, while an older threaded pipe behind plaster can require more careful removal. The age of the fitting also matters when corrosion has spread beyond the first wet joint.

A seized house shutoff can add work before we can safely isolate the leaking line. Drywall, tile, cabinets, insulation, and a pipe route inside a ceiling can all affect how much access we need to create and restore. If a valve, specialty fitting, or matching pipe material must be ordered, or if the repair needs an inspection sign-off, we explain that before scheduling the next part of the work.

Pipe material and access panel

Before calling, note which room has the leak and whether it sits beneath a sink, behind a refrigerator, near a washing machine, or above a ceiling fixture. If you can see the pipe, tell us whether it appears to be copper, white or tan plastic, gray plastic, or flexible PEX tubing. For a leak near a pressure regulator, filtration housing, or manifold, read the model number from the label or plate if it remains dry and accessible.

Let us know whether water is still running, whether the house shutoff works, and whether an access panel, cabinet back, crawl space, or attic reaches the pipe. Photos of the stain, wet cabinet floor, exposed fitting, or access opening can help us plan the right fittings and tools.
